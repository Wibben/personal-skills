# Zinsser's four principles

William Zinsser, *On Writing Well* (1976, 30th anniversary edition 2006),
names four principles of good nonfiction: **clarity, simplicity, brevity, and
humanity**. This file states what each one means for technical text, and how
each one fits with STE.

## Clarity

> "Clear thinking becomes clear writing; one can't exist without the other."

- Know what you think before you write. If you cannot state the point of a
  paragraph in one sentence, the problem is the thinking. Fix the thinking
  before you fix the sentence.
- Every sentence has one reading. This is also the core goal of STE.
- Put the point first. A reader who stops after the first sentence of a
  section must still have the main fact.
- Use concrete nouns and specific numbers. "The build takes 4 minutes", not
  "the build takes a while".

## Simplicity

> "The secret of good writing is to strip every sentence to its cleanest
> components."

- Use the short, common word: "use", not "utilize". "Start", not
  "commence". "Help", not "facilitate".
- Replace clutter phrases:

  | Clutter | Plain |
  |---|---|
  | in order to | to |
  | due to the fact that | because |
  | at this point in time | now, or delete |
  | prior to | before |
  | in the event that | if |
  | is able to | can |
  | a number of | some, or the number |
  | whether or not | whether |
  | for the purpose of | for, or to |
  | with regard to | about |

- Delete words that qualify nothing: "actually", "basically", "really",
  "very", "quite", "simply", "just", "note that", "it is important to".
- One idea per sentence. This matches the STE rule.

## Brevity

> "Look for the clutter in your writing and prune it ruthlessly."

- Cut every word that does no work. Then cut every sentence that does no
  work. Then check whether the paragraph does any work.
- Do not say the same thing twice. State each fact once, where the reader
  needs it.
- **Brevity stops at clarity.** This limit comes from the upstream STE skill,
  and it wins. Do not drop an article, a subject, a qualifier, or a hedge to
  save a word. A short sentence that a reader can misread is not brief. It is
  broken.

## Humanity

> "Writing is an intimate transaction between two people, conducted on
> paper, and it will go well to the extent that it retains its humanity."

- Write as one knowledgeable person explaining something to another. Ask
  how a good colleague would say it at a whiteboard.
- Use the reader's words, not your in-group jargon. If a plain term exists,
  use it. If the jargon is necessary, define it once.
- Tell the reader *why*, not only *what*. A rule with its reason is easier to
  follow and to apply to a case the rule does not cover.
- Anticipate the next question and answer it in place.
- No corporate voice. No pomposity. No false modesty. Do not hide behind the
  passive voice or behind "it is believed that".
- **In Strict mode**, humanity means respect for the reader's time and
  context: complete conditions, no surprises, and no assumed knowledge. It
  does not mean personality. STE text stays flat. The warmth goes into what
  you choose to explain, not into the words.
